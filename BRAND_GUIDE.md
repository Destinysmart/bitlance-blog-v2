# Bitlance Brand Messaging Guide

A reference for writing Bitlance content (blog posts, newsletters, social, in-app copy) that actually sounds like Bitlance — built from the voice already established on bitlance.work.

## The Core Voice

Bitlance's homepage voice is **short, confident, and plain**. No fluff, no hype-adjectives, no corporate softening. It reads like a product built by people who use it, not a marketing team performing enthusiasm.

**Tagline pattern:** "Work Online. Get Paid in Bitcoin." — two short declarative sentences, no connecting words, each one a complete thought.

**Positioning line:** "The simplest freelance platform built for the Bitcoin economy." — plain claim, no adjectives stacked on adjectives.

**Sign-off line:** "Built for Bitcoiners, by Bitcoiners." — community-first, not corporate.

## Sentence Rhythm

Pull directly from the site, Bitlance sentences are short and mostly declarative. Compare:

| Bitlance says | Bitlance does NOT say |
|---|---|
| "Fast and low-fee payments via Lightning Network." | "Experience lightning-fast payments powered by cutting-edge Lightning Network technology." |
| "Funds are secured until work is approved." | "Rest assured, your funds remain safeguarded throughout the entire transaction lifecycle." |
| "We keep platform fees minimal so you earn more." | "Our commitment to affordability ensures freelancers maximize their earning potential." |

The pattern: **one clear claim per sentence, plain verbs, no filler.** If a sentence could be cut in half and still make sense, cut it.

## Vocabulary

**Use these terms, matching the site's actual usage:**
- "sats" (not "Bitcoin satoshis" or "satoshi units")
- "Lightning Network" / "Lightning payments" (capitalized, as a proper noun)
- "Bitcoin-native," "Bitcoin economy," "Bitcoiners" — these are Bitlance's identity words
- "escrow protection" (not "payment security system")
- "global talent pool," "borderless," "worldwide" — used for reach

**Avoid:**
- "crypto" as a stand-in for Bitcoin (the site never uses "crypto" — it's Bitcoin-specific, not blockchain-general)
- Generic freelance-platform jargon like "gig economy," "workforce solutions," "talent acquisition"
- Hype words: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "cutting-edge," "seamless" (none of these appear anywhere on the site)
- Long feature descriptions. If Bitlance's own feature blurbs are one sentence, blog content explaining the same feature shouldn't need three paragraphs of throat-clearing before getting there

## Structural Patterns to Reuse

**Numbered/stepped explanations** (from "How Bitlance Works"):
1. Short bolded step title
2. One-sentence plain explanation

**Stat callouts** (from the hero section):
Short number + short label. "200+ Active Jobs." "98% Success Rate." No sentence needed, the number does the work.

**Feature grid pattern** (from "Built for the Bitcoin Native"):
Bolded 2-3 word feature name, followed by one plain sentence. Never more than one sentence per feature.

**Testimonial voice:**
First-person, casual, short. "Finally a platform that understands the Bitcoin economy." Real people talking, not polished marketing quotes.

## What This Means for Blog Content

If an AI (or a writer) is producing content that feels "off-brand," it's likely doing one or more of these things Bitlance's actual voice never does:

- Opening with a throat-clearing intro paragraph before getting to the point
- Using words like "seamless," "empower," "unlock," "elevate," "revolutionize"
- Writing long, compound sentences with multiple clauses stacked together
- Explaining Bitcoin/Lightning concepts as if the reader is a total beginner who needs hand-holding (the site's own copy assumes a Bitcoin-literate audience, it says "sats" and "Lightning Network" with zero explanation)
- Sounding neutral/generic enough that it could apply to any freelance platform, not specifically a Bitcoin-native one

## Quick Reference: Voice in One Line

**Plain, confident, Bitcoin-literate, no fluff.** Bitlance talks like someone who already gets it, talking to other people who already get it, not like a platform trying to convince skeptics.
